# Course Guide

## Daily default: 22 minutes

1. **Recall — 3 min:** identify notation before revealing the answer.
2. **Read — 8 min:** preview briefly, then perform easy unfamiliar material without stopping.
3. **Coach — 6 min:** use TD-07 TIME CHECK or QUIET COUNT for timing feedback.
4. **Piano bridge — 5 min:** map landmark pitches and intervals to a keyboard.

## Eight-week path

| Week | Module | Main outcome | TD-07 mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pulse & Measures | Maintain pulse through basic 4/4 | TIME CHECK |
| 2 | Eighth Notes & Rests | Count subdivisions through silence | TIME CHECK / QUIET COUNT |
| 3 | Meter & Ties | Read 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 and held values | QUIET COUNT |
| 4 | Syncopation | Recognize and perform offbeats | QUIET COUNT / AUTO UP-DOWN |
| 5 | Sixteenth Notes | Decode 1-e-&-a patterns | CHANGE UP |
| 6 | Drum-Set Reading | Layer hi-hat, snare, and bass | TIME CHECK |
| 7 | Fluent Reading | Read ahead and recover without stopping | QUIET COUNT |
| 8 | Piano Bridge | Use landmarks and intervals on grand staff | Virtual keyboard |

## Difficulty rule

Raise only one variable at a time: tempo, notation complexity, or limb coordination. If pulse repeatedly breaks, lower one variable. A useful promotion threshold is keeping time through roughly four of five easy examples.

## Sight-reading versus rehearsal

- **Sight-reading:** one short preview, one uninterrupted attempt, at most one retry, then new material.
- **Rehearsal:** repeat a known page to improve technique and polish.

Both matter. Do not let repeated repertoire replace unfamiliar reading.

## Suggested physical supplies

Required:

- Roland TD-07 kit or a pad/tabletop
- Drumsticks
- Headphones or safe amplification
- Pencil and printed or digital exercises
- Metronome (the TD-07 is sufficient)

Useful later:

- Blank staff paper
- Music stand
- Phone recording setup
- Printed keyboard map or browser piano
- Inexpensive velocity-sensitive MIDI keyboard when piano becomes a larger focus

## Drum-set notation house style

The portal uses a **common beginner drum-set layout**, not a claim that every drum chart has one mandatory map. On the five-line percussion staff:

- Regular **oval noteheads** represent snare, bass drum, and tom voices; their vertical position identifies the voice.
- **X-shaped noteheads** represent cymbal voices (closed hi-hat, ride, and crash in this course).
- A **vertical stack** means two or more targets happen at the same rhythmic instant; it is not written as a plus sign between notes.
- A dot is placed immediately after its notehead; a tie is an arc that joins two same-position noteheads into one attack.
- This course places snare around the middle of the staff, bass drum low, and cymbals above the staff. Other publishers may use different positions, voices/stems, clefs, or techniques. Always read the chart key.

The Percussive Arts Society describes its drum-set document as a survey and compendium of notation procedures currently used, rather than a universal mandate. Notation software also uses configurable drum maps: MuseScore’s kit documentation notes that individual sub-instruments’ notehead shapes and stave placement are configured and can be customized.

- PAS, [Guideline for Drumset Notation](https://pas.org/publication-articles/guideline-for-drumset-notation/)
- MuseScore Studio, [Inputting percussion notation](https://handbook.musescore.org/idiomatic-notation/percussion/inputting-percussion-notation)

## Evidence notes

The portal's design follows broad findings supporting retrieval and distributed practice, plus music-reading research emphasizing technical skill, auditory skill, music knowledge, experience, and efficient visual pattern processing. Direct sight-reading intervention research is more limited, so this project avoids unsupported numerical promises.
